# XXD Panel 090 Original Prompt · English Reading Translation

Retain the original photograph in the upper half, preserving the subject's identity, structure, pose, authentic texture, natural light and shadow, and original colour atmosphere. Instead, translate this information into a diagrammatic abstraction in the style of **Diagrammatic Abstraction / Conceptual Mapping**. Treat the photograph's core object as a “conceptual centre,” and break its action, direction, space, emotion, relationships, and metaphors down into words, nodes, trajectories, regions, arrows, and connections, as though directly drawing the thought process and structure of meaning behind the photograph.

Build the composition jointly through a **textual semantic system + geometric relationship system + handmade mark system**. Add a small number of contour-like curves, field lines, waveform trajectories, or paths around the periphery to create spatial relationships of diffusion, flow, convergence, and extension. Do not turn it into a standard flowchart or scientific diagram; preserve an artistic quality poised between painting, diagram, and thinking notes.

The image may incorporate many **repeated directional short lines, rhythmic strokes, dots, ticks, symbols, and local handwritten traces**, forming a mark-making field with varying directions and densities according to the subject's force, speed, emotion, or spatial density. Keep the geometric lines clear, sharp, and restrained, while allowing the handmade marks slight shifts, breaks, and irregularities, creating tension between rational structure and embodied brushwork. Preserve broad areas of whitespace so that relationships, directions, and information have ample room to breathe.

For the palette, extract and remix **the 1–3 most vibrant and meaningful colours** from the photograph above; do not copy the photograph's colours directly or sample them evenly. Colours should be bright, clean, and energetic without becoming oversaturated, creating a clear hierarchy through a large pale ground and a small amount of high-intensity colour. Avoid muddy greys, complex composite hues, fluorescent effects, and meaningless accumulations of many colours.

Text itself is a graphic structure; do not preset a fixed title, number, location, or language. Freely distil keywords, short phrases, conceptual fragments, or invented marks from the photograph's subject, action, relationships, emotion, sound, sense of time, or symbolic meaning, and make them participate in the composition through **contrasts of scale, rotation, repetition, overlap, arrangement along paths, partial fragmentation, and spatial connection**. The overall result should present an experimental visual language of **Visual Thinking × Concept Mapping × Diagrammatic Drawing × Experimental Typography × Visual Notation**: from a distance, it has clear geometric order and a distinct visual focus; up close, it is rich with relationships among text, trajectories, symbols, and handmade traces. Whether the original image depicts a person, animal, building, plant, object, vehicle, or natural landscape, do not turn it into an object-explanation diagram. Reinterpret it instead as a visual thinking map about **relationships, processes, states, and meaning**.

Avoid traditional mind maps, standard flowcharts, data dashboards, technology UI, cyber interfaces, regular grids that fill the canvas, decorative random lines, dense accumulations of complex information, realistic illustration, and templated infographics.
